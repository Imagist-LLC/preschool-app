The Science Behind Preschool.app

Parents often worry about screen time, especially for young children. But not all screen time is the same. Research shows that well-designed interactive educational apps can enhance early learning, improving memory, literacy, and math skills when used appropriately.

“Studies have found that when preschoolers actively tap, swipe, and respond to content, they form stronger memory traces and understanding.”

Unlike passive screen time (like TV or non-educational videos), interactive learning apps actively engage a child’s brain, reinforcing essential concepts through touch, sound, and problem-solving.

1. Research Shows the Right Kind of Digital Learning Works

A 2021 meta-analysis of 36 studies found that digital learning apps had an overall positive effect on literacy and math skills—with an average improvement of +0.30 standard deviations.

“Notably, effects were strongest for preschoolers, suggesting that ages 3–5 reap substantial gains from interactive app experiences.”

Another comprehensive review of ~5,000 children under age 6 found that the biggest improvements were in early math, followed by language and literacy skills.

🔹 Key Takeaway

Educational apps, when designed well, can match or even surpass some traditional teaching methods in effectiveness. But quality matters—which brings us to our next point.

2. Poorly Designed Apps Can Be Harmful

The 2021 meta-analysis also noted that its positive results may not apply to the tens of thousands of poorly designed apps on the market. Many so-called “educational” apps:

  • Contain excessive distractions (ads, pop-ups, unrelated games).
  • Offer low-quality engagement (random tapping with no real learning).
  • Use gimmicky rewards instead of structured reinforcement.

“Studies warn that too much interactivity or irrelevant multimedia can backfire, overstimulating children and reducing comprehension.”

✅ Our Approach: Research-Based, High-Quality Learning

  • ✔ Focus on core early learning (phonics, numbers, vocabulary).
  • ✔ Use interactive, hands-on engagement (not passive watching).
  • ✔ Provide clear, structured reinforcement (instead of random rewards).

3. Experts Recommend Using Educational Apps as a Supplement

“Experts recommend using educational apps to supplement traditional preschool activities, not to replace them entirely.”

Apps work best when combined with real-world learning:

  • ✔ Read books together while using digital phonics tools.
  • ✔ Practice counting on the app, then count real objects.
  • ✔ Encourage co-play—research shows children learn better when parents engage with them during screen time.

4. What About Screen Time Limits?

Many parents are concerned about screen time recommendations, especially for children under 5.

🔹 The AAP Has Updated Its Guidelines

The American Academy of Pediatrics (AAP) previously recommended no screen time before age 2, but this has since changed. The latest guidelines acknowledge that:

“Interactive, high-quality educational media can have benefits for young children when used in moderation and with parental guidance.”

5. Is Bilingual Learning Confusing for Young Children?

“Bilingual children develop stronger cognitive flexibility, problem-solving skills, and working memory compared to monolingual peers.”

A study on bilingual preschoolers found that dual-language exposure actually enhances vocabulary in both languages, rather than causing confusion.
